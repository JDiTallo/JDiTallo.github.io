A Brief Review of Never Let Me Go by Kazuo Ishiguro

by Jack DiTallo


7 January 2026


Midway last semester, I realized that while reading is a core part of my studies, I couldn’t say that I dedicate much time reading for leisure. With constant exams, I am often kept on my toes when it comes to studying. Yet during lunch breaks, I figured I should read something, anything.


It isn’t out of character for me to choose blindly, so when Never Let Me Go by Kazuo Ishiguro was recommended to me for its characters, I figured I should give it a shot.


I won’t get into major spoiler territory in this review, but instead I’ll lay the groundwork for my general impressions, though I do reference early plot points in the novel.


Overall, the novel kept me engaged with its rather simplistic scope and often “quiet” setting. There isn’t really any action, nor grand stakes at play. Rather, the novel delves into the perspective of Kathy H, a “carer” who handles overseeing people’s “donations,” as she reflects over her life.


She describes her experiences at Hailsham, an institution that has mysteriously isolated its students from the outside world. In this school, students often learn mainly art and literature. While the “guardians” say it is important for them to learn, it is not told to them in what way. Furthermore, many pieces produced by students (such as paintings, poetry, etc.) are collected by a mysterious “madame,” one that seems somewhat terrified of the students. It is also suggested that the students would never be able to live their lives like regular people, even in adulthood. It is these uncertainties that make the characters feel as though they lack clear understanding of their own identity.


Kathy’s life is nothing particularly special. But it is her memories of Hailsham, and later in her adult life that spark meaning. She must grapple with who she really is and has to overcome changes in relationships she used to consider lifelong. This is rather a coming-of-age story with a dystopian twist


While I don’t often read much for leisure, this novel was a good pairing in between classes. Like its characters, we ourselves cannot control the world, nor do we know the complete truth about it. At times, perhaps it is important to bring focus to aspects of life we can appreciate and enjoy.
