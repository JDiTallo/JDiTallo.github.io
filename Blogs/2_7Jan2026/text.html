A Brief Review of 4' 33" by John Cage

by Jack DiTallo

7 January 2026

Originally written on 25 November 2025

This piece is quite misunderstood. At the end of the day, it truly resembles life itself. It doesn’t require instruments or anything really. Instead, it merely requires the innate ability to listen and/or perceive, a feat anyone can do. Really, this piece has been played before John Cage was even born. This song existed before the creation of music. Calling the song 4’ 33” is a great misnomer, because this piece is about the absence of “traditional” music. Also, the time frame doesn’t represent the song in its entirety. It is more accurate to call the piece ∞, as it never truly has an end. But does it have a beginning? Before there was a thing called earth, or a universe, had this song been playing before even the creation of anything? That is a good question, because we could never truly know. Is the piece over when we, the people perceiving it, pass away? If this piece resembles everything, could one say that the piece is over then there is nothing at all? But is there even a time and a place where there is nothing? Perhaps the title is more accurate in the sense that it is not about the entirety of the song. Maybe it is an excerpt of the limited amount of time we have on this earth, how the portion of the music we listen to will never be able to the played again.

John Cage’s composition, unlike other compositions, has nothing to do with the song itself. In a way, we have all listened to this song, even daily, when we go to bed for the night. And the song and its experience on each person is much less about the composition, and more about what someone does when confronted with silence. In a world where there is constant distraction no matter where you go, it is pieces like this that highlight a growing rarity. In my “performances” of this piece, I tend to notice the humming of the AC unit, or the blowing of a fan, or even the distance sounds of my family. Perhaps in even more secluded parts of the house, I hear myself thinking about a certain math formula or even thinking about other songs that I have stuck in my head. Sometimes, I have internal dialogue about certain physics problems, or I simply relax and enjoy the present moment. I guess the twist is, is that the song is about paying attention to the present moment, because it really isn’t about a grand event, but rather the little things that are suddenly the only thing one can focus on.
